# Legal Maxims Seed (Indian Legal Context)

This seed list contains common Latin legal maxims, plain-English meaning, and a short usage example.

1. **Actus non facit reum nisi mens sit rea** - An act does not make a person guilty unless there is a guilty mind. - Example: In criminal trials, prosecution must show intent or knowledge where required.
2. **Actori incumbit onus probandi** - The burden of proof lies on the claimant. - Example: A plaintiff alleging breach must prove the breach.
3. **Audi alteram partem** - Hear the other side. - Example: Administrative orders passed without hearing affected parties can be set aside.
4. **Nemo judex in causa sua** - No one should be a judge in their own cause. - Example: A biased authority must recuse from adjudication.
5. **Ignorantia juris non excusat** - Ignorance of law is no excuse. - Example: A person cannot avoid liability by claiming not to know the statute.
6. **Res ipsa loquitur** - The thing speaks for itself. - Example: In negligence, certain accidents imply negligence without direct proof.
7. **Mens rea** - Guilty mind. - Example: Intention is relevant in many penal provisions.
8. **Actus reus** - Guilty act. - Example: Physical commission of the prohibited act must be shown.
9. **Volenti non fit injuria** - To a willing person, no injury is done. - Example: Consent can be a defense in limited tort contexts.
10. **Ubi jus ibi remedium** - Where there is a right, there is a remedy. - Example: Constitutional courts grant relief when legal rights are violated.
11. **De minimis non curat lex** - The law does not concern itself with trifles. - Example: Courts may ignore insignificant technical lapses.
12. **Injuria sine damno** - Violation of a legal right without actual damage. - Example: Action may lie if a right is infringed even without monetary loss.
13. **Damnum sine injuria** - Damage without legal injury. - Example: Business loss due to lawful competition is not actionable.
14. **Ex turpi causa non oritur actio** - No action arises from an immoral or illegal cause. - Example: Courts refuse to enforce contracts founded on illegality.
15. **Qui facit per alium facit per se** - He who acts through another acts himself. - Example: Principal may be liable for authorized acts of an agent.
16. **Res judicata** - A matter already adjudicated cannot be re-litigated. - Example: Final judgments bar repeat litigation on same issues.
17. **Lis pendens** - Pending litigation binds transfer of disputed property. - Example: Transfer during suit remains subject to final decree.
18. **Caveat emptor** - Let the buyer beware. - Example: Purchaser must inspect quality unless statutory protections apply.
19. **Caveat venditor** - Let the seller beware. - Example: Consumer law imposes duties on sellers against unfair trade.
20. **Pacta sunt servanda** - Agreements must be kept. - Example: Courts enforce valid contracts as per terms.
21. **Consensus ad idem** - Meeting of minds. - Example: Contract fails if parties never agreed on same terms.
22. **Expressio unius est exclusio alterius** - Express mention of one excludes others. - Example: Specific statutory inclusion may imply exclusion of non-listed items.
23. **Generalia specialibus non derogant** - General law does not override special law. - Example: Special statute prevails over general procedural provision.
24. **Lex specialis derogat legi generali** - Special law prevails over general law. - Example: Special anti-corruption procedure controls over general code provisions.
25. **Lex posterior derogat priori** - Later law overrides earlier inconsistent law. - Example: New penal code provisions can displace older conflicting sections.
26. **Salus populi suprema lex** - Welfare of the people is the supreme law. - Example: Public health restrictions may be justified in emergencies.
27. **Delegatus non potest delegare** - A delegate cannot further delegate. - Example: Statutory authority cannot sub-delegate without enabling power.
28. **Falsus in uno, falsus in omnibus** - False in one thing, false in all. - Example: In India, this is a cautionary rule, not rigidly applied.
29. **Nemo dat quod non habet** - No one can transfer what they do not have. - Example: Seller without title cannot pass valid title, subject to exceptions.
30. **Nullum crimen sine lege** - No crime without law. - Example: Criminal liability requires prior legal prohibition.
31. **Nulla poena sine lege** - No punishment without law. - Example: Penalty cannot be imposed unless prescribed by law.
32. **In dubio pro reo** - When in doubt, favor the accused. - Example: Benefit of doubt leads to acquittal in criminal trials.
33. **Bona fide** - In good faith. - Example: Good-faith actions by public servants may attract statutory protection.
34. **Mala fide** - In bad faith. - Example: Orders passed with malice can be quashed in judicial review.
35. **Pro bono publico** - For public good. - Example: Public-interest interventions often invoke broader social benefit.
36. **Prima facie** - At first appearance. - Example: Court may frame charge if prima facie case exists.
37. **Per incuriam** - Through lack of due care. - Example: A precedent rendered in ignorance of binding law may be per incuriam.
38. **Stare decisis** - Stand by decided matters. - Example: Coordinate benches follow binding precedent for consistency.
39. **Ratio decidendi** - The binding principle of a decision. - Example: Only ratio, not every observation, has precedential force.
40. **Obiter dicta** - Incidental remarks in a judgment. - Example: Obiter may guide but is generally not binding.
41. **Casus omissus** - A case omitted from statute. - Example: Courts avoid supplying omissions unless necessary and clear.
42. **Ejusdem generis** - Of the same kind. - Example: General words after specific words are read in same class.
43. **Noscitur a sociis** - A word is known by its associates. - Example: Ambiguous terms are interpreted from surrounding words.
44. **Ut res magis valeat quam pereat** - Interpret to make statute effective, not futile. - Example: Courts prefer construction preserving legislative purpose.
45. **Contemporanea expositio** - Contemporary interpretation has weight. - Example: Long-standing administrative interpretation may be considered.
46. **Fraus et jus nunquam cohabitant** - Fraud and justice never dwell together. - Example: Fraud vitiates judicial and administrative proceedings.
47. **He who seeks equity must do equity** - Equitable relief requires fair conduct by claimant. - Example: Injunction may be denied if claimant acted unfairly.
48. **He who comes into equity must come with clean hands** - Equitable claimant must be free of misconduct. - Example: Suppression of facts can defeat discretionary relief.
49. **Delay defeats equity** - Unreasonable delay can bar equitable relief. - Example: Laches may defeat stale writ claims.
50. **Actus curiae neminem gravabit** - An act of court shall prejudice no one. - Example: Parties should not suffer due to court-caused procedural delay.
